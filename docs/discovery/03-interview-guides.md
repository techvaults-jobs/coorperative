# 3. Stakeholder Interview Guides

## 3.1 How to run interviews

- **Format:** 45–60 minutes, ideally in person and where the work happens (the treasurer's office, the bursary). Two people from TechVaults: one asks, one takes notes.
- **Consent first:** read the consent script (§3.11). Record audio only with explicit consent. Never photograph a document showing member names, account numbers, salaries or NINs. Ask for an anonymised copy instead ([05](05-sample-collection-template.md)).
- **Ask about the past, not the hypothetical** (Teresa Torres, *Continuous Discovery Habits*). "Tell me about the last time a member retired" beats "How would you like retirement to work?"
- **Ask them to show you.** "Can you show me the spreadsheet you used last month?" Watch for workarounds, copying between files, and calculators.
- **Don't pitch.** Don't show a product or describe features until the final 5 minutes, if at all.
- **Log each answer with its source.** Every finding is [STAKEHOLDER: society ID, role]. One society's practice is never "how cooperatives work".
- **Same-day synthesis:** fill in the interview snapshot (§3.12) within 24 hours.

## 3.2 Opening questions (all roles, 5 minutes)

1. What is your role in the society, how long have you held it, and is it paid or voluntary?
2. Roughly how many members does the society have, and how has that changed?
3. Walk me through what you did for the society last week.
4. Which part of your cooperative work takes the most time? Which part worries you most?

## 3.3 Chairperson, executive committee, principal officer

1. How are decisions made: committee meetings, AGM, delegated officers? How often do you meet?
2. Which decisions need committee approval, and which need AGM approval? Are there value thresholds?
3. Tell me about the last bye-law or policy change. How was it proposed, approved and communicated?
4. How do you know the society's finances are healthy? What reports do you look at, and how often?
5. What do the State Department of Cooperatives or your auditors ask you for, and when?
6. Have you started the national revalidation (NCSR, CVN)? What has the experience been like?
7. What complaints do members bring to you most often?
8. Describe the last dispute involving a member, a loan or a guarantor. How was it resolved?
9. How are elections held, and how are signatories changed afterwards?
10. Has the society ever discovered fraud or a serious error? How was it found?
11. Who chose your current tools (books, Excel, software)? What would make you change them? Who would decide, and with what budget?

## 3.4 Secretary or administrator

1. Walk me through how a new member joins, from first enquiry to their first deduction.
2. What forms and documents do you collect? Can we see a blank copy?
3. How are member numbers assigned? What happens to the number when someone leaves and rejoins?
4. How do you find out that a member has changed department, been transferred, retired or died?
5. How do you keep records up to date (address, next of kin, bank account)? Who is allowed to change them?
6. Have you ever found a duplicate member, or someone deducted who wasn't a member?
7. How do you communicate with members: notices, WhatsApp, SMS, email?
8. How are minutes, resolutions and correspondence stored?
9. What files would you grab first in a fire?

## 3.5 Treasurer or accountant

1. Show me how you close a month. What do you start with and what do you produce?
2. What accounting records do you keep: cashbook, ledgers, journals, trial balance? In what tool?
3. How many bank accounts does the society have, and who can sign?
4. When the payroll remittance arrives, how do you match it to individual members? How long does that take?
5. What happens when the remittance is short, late, or includes people who aren't members?
6. How do you split each member's deduction between savings, shares, loan principal, interest and other items? Who decides the order?
7. How do you calculate loan interest? Show me an example of a real (anonymised) loan.
8. How do you produce a member's statement? How often do members ask for one?
9. Describe your bank reconciliation process. How often is it done, and by whom?
10. How do you correct a mistake once it has been entered?
11. How are year-end accounts prepared, who audits them, and what do auditors usually query?
12. How are dividends or surplus computed and paid? Can we see last year's computation?
13. What investments, fixed assets or trading activities does the society have?
14. What are the payment approval steps: vouchers, signatories, limits?

## 3.6 Loan officer or loan committee member

1. What loan products do you offer? For each: maximum amount, term, interest rate and method, fees, eligibility.
2. Walk me through the last loan approved, from application to disbursement. How many days did it take?
3. How do you check eligibility (months of membership, savings multiple, salary capacity, existing loans)?
4. How do guarantors work? How many are required, what must they have, can a person guarantee several loans, and what happens to the guarantor if the borrower defaults?
5. Who approves which loans? Are there limits by amount or by who the applicant is (for example, a committee member)?
6. How do you check that the employer's payroll can still take the deduction (the net-pay floor)?
7. How is a loan disbursed: cheque, transfer, offset against another loan?
8. What happens with top-ups, refinancing, rescheduling and early repayment?
9. How do you track arrears? What is done at 1, 3 and 6 months overdue?
10. Tell me about the last loan written off or recovered from guarantors or savings.
11. How do you handle loans to someone due to retire within the loan term?
12. Are there queues or rationing because funds run short? How are applicants prioritised?

## 3.7 Payroll or HR officer (employer side)

1. How does the cooperative's deduction get onto the payroll? Who sends what, in what format, and by what date?
2. Which payroll system do you use (IPPIS, state platform, SAP, Sage, a bespoke HR system)? Can we see a blank deduction template?
3. How do you handle a deduction that exceeds the member's net pay or a statutory cap?
4. How and when is money remitted to the cooperative? Is there a remittance schedule?
5. What do you send back to the cooperative: a deduction report, exceptions?
6. How do you notify the cooperative of resignation, retirement, transfer or death?
7. What problems come up between payroll and the cooperative?
8. What data-sharing agreements or approvals exist between employer and cooperative?

## 3.8 Internal or external auditor

1. Which framework and standards do you audit cooperatives against?
2. What records do you ask for? Which are most often missing or inconsistent?
3. How do you test member balances, the loan portfolio and interest income?
4. What are your most common findings and management-letter points?
5. What do you need from a software system to rely on it (audit trail, change logs, read-only access, exports)?
6. What do you file with the State Department, and when?
7. How do you treat member shares, savings, loan impairment and reserves?

## 3.9 Members (including recently retired or exiting members)

1. Why did you join? What do you use the cooperative for?
2. How do you know how much you have saved and how much you owe? When did you last check?
3. Tell me about the last time you applied for a loan. What was hard? How long did it take?
4. Have you ever been a guarantor? How did that go?
5. Have you ever disagreed with your balance or a deduction? What happened?
6. *(Retired or exited)* Walk me through leaving the society. How long did settlement take? Were you satisfied with the calculation?
7. What phone do you use? Do you use banking apps, USSD, WhatsApp?
8. Who do you trust in the society, and why?

## 3.10 Closing (all roles)

1. If you could fix one thing about how the society runs, what would it be?
2. Is there anything we should have asked?
3. Who else should we talk to? (Ask for introductions.)
4. Could you share anonymised examples of the documents we discussed? (Use the checklist in [05](05-sample-collection-template.md).)

## 3.11 Consent script (summary)

> "We're from TechVaults. We're researching how cooperative societies operate so we can design better tools. There are no right or wrong answers. You can skip any question or stop at any time. With your permission we'll take notes [and record audio]. We will not publish your name or your society's name without written permission. We will not collect member personal data. Any documents you share should be blank or anonymised. Do you agree to continue?"

Record: date, society ID (pseudonym), role, consent given (Y/N), recording consent (Y/N), documents promised.
