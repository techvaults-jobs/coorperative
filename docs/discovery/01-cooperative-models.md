# 1. Cooperative Models to Study in Nigeria

The rows below are **[HYPOTHESIS]**. They are working descriptions built from the TechVaults brief and general sector knowledge. Interviews must confirm or correct each cell. Do not cite this table as fact.

## 1.2 Cross-cutting dimensions to record for every society

Classify every interviewed society on these axes, not just its segment label. Our configurable product will vary along the axes, not the labels.

1. **Collection channel:** payroll deduction, bank transfer, cash, agent, USSD or mobile money, or a mix.
2. **Employer relationship:** none, employer facilitates deductions, employer contributes, or employer guarantees.
3. **Legal registration:** state-registered, federally registered, or unregistered. Also NCSR revalidation status (CVN issued?).
4. **Size:** members, branches, annual turnover, loan book.
5. **Product breadth:** savings only, savings and loans, multipurpose (trading, investments, welfare).
6. **Share capital model:** fixed shares, variable shares, none.
7. **Surplus model:** dividends on shares, patronage refund, interest rebate, none.
8. **Governance formality:** staffed secretariat and external auditor, or volunteer officers.
9. **Current tooling:** paper, Excel, WhatsApp, accounting package, cooperative software.
10. **Digital literacy and device access** among officers and members.

## 1.3 Hypothesised commonalities (to test)

- **[HYPOTHESIS]** Segments A–D share one operating core: a monthly deduction cycle, a member ledger, loans backed by guarantors and savings multiples, and settlement on exit. If so, they are one product with configuration, not four products.
- **[HYPOTHESIS]** Segments F and G differ most. They involve irregular cash collection, group liability and field agents. They probably need a different collection module and a different mobile experience, even if the ledger core is shared.
- **[HYPOTHESIS]** Segment H is mostly A–D with branch accounting, investments and trading modules added.

## 1.4 Recommended discovery sample (8–12 societies)

| Segment | Target interviews | Rationale |
|---|---|---|
| A University | 2–3 | Likely early market, many loan products, retirement-heavy |
| B Ministry or agency | 2 | Payroll-platform complexity, large registers |
| C Hospital | 1–2 | Turnover and multi-source payroll stress-tests reconciliation |
| D Corporate | 2 | HR-system integration, likely ability to pay |
| E/F/G/H | 2–3 (at least one of F or G, one H) | Prevent the core design from becoming payroll-only by accident |

Aim for spread across at least **three states**, including Lagos and the FCT, plus one other state. State laws and supervisory practice may differ ([02](02-regulatory-landscape-and-sources.md)).
