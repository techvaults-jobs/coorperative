### Provisions of Cap N98 (added 17 Oct 2026)

Read from the [FAOLEX copy](https://faolex.fao.org/docs/pdf/nig120579.pdf) (Decree 1993 No. 90, commenced 26 Aug 1993) and cross-checked with the [Jet Lawyer copy](https://djetlawyer.com/nigerian-co-operative-societies-act/). **[VERIFIED: text]**. What the text means, and how it applies alongside state laws, still needs counsel.

| # | Provision | Product implication [HYPOTHESIS] |
|---|---|---|
| F17 | **s.1:** the President appoints a Federal Director of Co-operatives (s.1(1)). **A State Governor may appoint a State Director of Co-operatives** (s.1(2)). | The Act itself provides for directors at both federal and state level. The system must record which Director (registrar) a society answers to. |
| F18 | **s.3(1):** a primary society needs **at least ten persons**. s.3(2): an industrial society needs at least six. | This conflicts with the "20 for savings and credit" in practitioner guides (F12). That figure may come from state law or regulations. **OPEN.** |
| F19 | **s.27:** no member, other than a registered society, may hold **more than one fifth of share capital**. | A share-capital cap (20%) that the system enforces by default. |
| F20 | **s.16:** the society has a **charge on the shares, interests and deposits** of present, past and deceased members for debts owed to it, and may **set off** amounts owed against dividends, bonuses or profits payable. | Supports netting loans against shares and deposits at exit. The exact order and scope should come from bye-laws plus counsel. |
| F21 | **s.18:** on a member's death, their interest goes to the **nominee**. If there is none, it goes to the **heir or legal representative**. The society may instead pay out the value. | The member record needs a nominee designation, kept separate from next of kin. Death settlement has a statutory order of recipients. |
| F22 | **s.30:** the society may **not lend to non-members**, except another registered society with majority member consent. **s.32:** other dealings with non-members are subject to prescribed restrictions. | Loans may only go to active members. Exited members with residual loans need a defined treatment. |
| F23 | **s.36:** accounts are **audited at each financial year-end** by an auditor **approved in writing**. The Director may prescribe returns (s.36(7)) and has access to all books (s.36(6)). | Year-end close workflow, an auditor role with read access, and a returns generator driven by Director directions (state-specific). |
| F24 | **s.49:** certain disputes go **to the Director** for settlement or arbitration. Appeal to the Minister or Commissioner within 30 days. | A dispute register with an escalation path. |
| F25 | **s.11:** bye-laws must cover member withdrawal and expulsion and any payment due to them, and member loans (interest, maximum amounts, repayment periods). | Confirms that **exit payouts and loan terms are bye-law-defined, so they are configurable policy**, within statutory guardrails. |
| F26 | The Act contains **no provision on salary or payroll deductions**. | Payroll deduction rests on employer arrangements and member authorisation, not the Act. Collect the deduction authority (D05). |
| F27 | **IPPIS** (OAGF) handles third-party remittances, and cooperatives are one listed category. Reports describe the HR role player creating the cooperative on IPPIS before deductions can start. Academic work reports **poor coordination** between cooperatives and the IPPIS office on recovering loans. | **REPORTED.** Supports keeping federal-payroll societies out of the first pilot. Get the official procedure from OAGF. Sources: [Guardian](https://guardian.ng/opinion/ippis-intrigues-technicalities-in-nigerian-varsities/), [ResearchGate study](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/355168390_Internal_Security_Management_of_Funds_and_IPPIS_Challenges_among_Unions_and_Cooperative_Societies_in_Tertiary_Institutions_in_Nigeria) |

**Note on age:** the Act's penalty figures (e.g. s.34(4)) date from 1993, which is one reason a repeal is under way (F4–F5). Do not build penalty logic into the product.

### Product implications (all [HYPOTHESIS] until legal review)
- **Reserve-fund rule (F2):** dividend and surplus logic needs a statutory minimum reserve (25%) that the platform enforces, with a recorded Director exemption as the only override. Societies should not be able to configure it away. Also needed: an optional education-fund appropriation capped at 10%, and a Minister or Commissioner approval step for unlimited-liability societies.
- **NCSR (F7–F9):** every society record should store its CVN. Member records should store a CoopID field. NIN storage should be optional and justified under NDPA. A future NCSR integration should be designed for but not built until an API is published.
- **CBN (F15):** the platform must stay a **record-keeping and administration tool**. It must not take deposits from non-members, and money movement must go through licensed providers.

## 2.3 Sector organisations and standards

- **International Cooperative Alliance (ICA)** and **ICA-Africa**: principles, Model Cooperative Law (the stated reference for Nigeria's reform). [ica.coop](https://ica.coop)
- **ILO**: Recommendation 193 (Promotion of Cooperatives), cooperative statistics guidelines.
- **WOCCU**: PEARLS financial monitoring ratios, credit-union governance and the *Model Law for Credit Unions*. Useful for defining reports and risk indicators. [woccu.org](https://www.woccu.org)
- **ICAN, ACCA, IFRS Foundation**: accounting treatment of member shares (IFRIC 2 *Members' Shares in Co-operative Entities* is directly relevant), loan impairment (IFRS 9 or simplified approaches) and SME reporting.
- **OWASP (ASVS, Top 10), CIS Controls**: security baseline.

## 2.5 Federal vs state: how to resolve the question

We could not confirm from primary sources how the Constitution divides cooperative-law powers between federal and state governments, or how far the federal Act binds state-registered societies. **[OPEN]** The Act itself provides for State Directors (F17), and states such as Lagos have their own laws (F11). That suggests both layers operate. How they rank against each other is a question for counsel. Steps:
1. Get the current text of Cap N98 and of state laws for Lagos, the FCT and one other state.
2. Ask counsel how the Act and state laws relate, and which law governs a society registered in state X whose employer is federal.
3. Record the answer as a jurisdiction matrix covering registration, audit, annual returns, reserve rules, dispute resolution and liquidation.

## 2.6 Questions requiring expert review

**Legal (Nigerian cooperative and financial-services counsel)**
1. Which law governs a society: the federal Act or the state law? How do they interact?
2. Statutory minimum reserve, distribution approvals, audit requirements (who may audit, how often, filing deadline).
3. Is taking savings from members only clearly outside CBN licensing? What about non-member deposits or online collection?
4. Can loan interest be charged freely, and is there any usury or disclosure requirement?
5. Settlement on death: what must be paid to the nominee or next of kin, and what needs probate or letters of administration?
6. Legal status of electronic records, e-signatures and electronic AGM votes under current law (Evidence Act 2011 s.84; Cybercrimes Act).
7. Required record-retention periods.

**Accounting (ICAN/ACCA-qualified cooperative auditor)**
8. Are members' shares equity or a liability (IFRIC 2)? Should withdrawable savings be shown as liabilities?
9. Interest recognition methods (flat, reducing balance, upfront deduction) and how to present them.
10. Loan impairment and provisioning approach expected by state auditors.
11. Chart of accounts standard used by state audit departments.
12. Dividend and patronage-refund computation and period cut-off.

**Data protection and security (NDPC-registered DPO or compliance organisation)**
13. Are TechVaults and each society data controllers, processors or joint controllers?
14. Is a DPIA required (financial data at scale, NIN)? Do we fall in the GAID audit-return tiers?
15. Is data localisation required, and what are the cross-border hosting rules?
16. Lawful basis for processing next-of-kin and guarantor data.
